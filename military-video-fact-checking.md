# AVIATION & MILITARY TECHNOLOGY VIDEO FACT-CHECKING MASTER PROMPT — V3.0
Analyze the uploaded video in full.
Your task is to perform a **professional technical fact-check, open-source verification, evidence audit, visual consistency check, and logical argument analysis** of everything stated, shown, or technically implied in the video.
The objective is NOT to prove the video wrong.
The objective is to determine:
* What exactly does the video claim?
* What can be independently verified?
* What is contradicted?
* What is only partially correct?
* What is outdated?
* What is dependent on configuration, variant, mission or timeframe?
* What is merely an interpretation or opinion?
* What remains uncertain?
* Does the video's final conclusion actually follow from the available evidence?
Use current, publicly available open sources whenever external verification is required.

# 4. DISTINGUISH CLAIM, FACT, OBSERVATION AND INFERENCE
For every relevant statement, distinguish between:
### EXPLICIT CLAIM
What the video directly states.
### VISUAL OBSERVATION
What can actually be observed in the footage.
### DOCUMENTED FACT
Information independently supported by reliable evidence.
### INFERENCE
A conclusion that can reasonably be derived from available evidence but is not directly confirmed.
### INTERPRETATION
An analytical conclusion made by the video's author, source or another party.
### UNCERTAINTY
A point for which reliable public evidence is insufficient.
Never convert an inference, interpretation or visual observation into an established fact.

# 9. SOURCE INDEPENDENCE & EVIDENCE CHAIN
Do not count multiple websites as independent confirmation if they simply repeat the same original source.
Whenever possible, trace:
**ORIGINAL SOURCE → SECONDARY REPORTING → VIDEO CLAIM**
Identify the earliest reliable source available.
If several publications repeat the same unsupported statement, treat them as one underlying evidence chain rather than multiple independent confirmations.
For OSINT claims, identify the evidence type where relevant:
* Satellite imagery
* Official imagery
* Photographic evidence
* Video evidence
* Leaked documents
* Procurement records
* Test reports
* Flight tracking
* Geolocation
* Specialist analysis
Explain important limitations of the evidence.

# 10. CROSS-CHECK EVERY IMPORTANT CLAIM
For significant technical claims:
1. Identify the exact claim.
2. Search for the strongest available source.
3. Find the original or primary source where possible.
4. Look for independent corroboration.
5. Check the exact variant.
6. Check the configuration.
7. Check the relevant timeframe.
8. Compare conflicting specifications.
9. Determine whether the claim is confirmed, disputed, outdated or unsupported.
10. Determine whether the source actually supports the video's wording.
Do NOT accept a claim simply because one website reports it.
When reliable sources disagree, explicitly report the disagreement.
Never silently select one number when credible sources provide different values.

# 11. CLAIM DEFINITION AUDIT
For controversial, comparative, classificatory or evaluative technical claims, do NOT fact-check only the final conclusion.
First identify the definition, criteria or technical standard being used.
Examples:
* 4th generation
* 4.5 generation
* 5th generation
* 6th generation
* Stealth
* Low observable
* Supercruise
* Sensor fusion
* Network-centric warfare
* Combat proven
* Hypersonic
* Autonomous
* Semi-autonomous
* Unmanned
* Optionally manned
* Multirole
* Beyond-visual-range capability
* Electronic warfare capability
## A. DEFINE THE TERM
Determine how credible:
* Military
* Government
* Industry
* Academic
* Technical
sources define the term.
Do not assume that a commonly used classification has one universally accepted definition.
## B. IDENTIFY THE VIDEO'S CRITERIA
Determine exactly which criteria the video uses to reach its conclusion.
Separate:
**VIDEO'S DEFINITION / CRITERIA**
from:
**EXTERNAL DEFINITIONS / CRITERIA**
## C. TEST EACH CRITERION
For every criterion:
1. Determine whether it is technically meaningful.
2. Determine whether credible sources support its use.
3. Determine whether the platform satisfies it.
4. Determine the strength of available evidence.
5. Determine whether the evidence applies to the exact variant.
6. Identify contradictory or incomplete evidence.
Do not allow a single disputed criterion to automatically determine the entire classification unless the source explicitly establishes that criterion as decisive.

# 12. ARGUMENT AND LOGICAL CONSISTENCY AUDIT
Determine whether the video's conclusion actually follows from the evidence it presents.
Check for:
* Unsupported assumptions
* Missing criteria
* Changing definitions
* Variant confusion
* Configuration confusion
* False equivalence
* Cherry-picked specifications
* Selective source usage
* Outdated information
* Marketing claims treated as technical facts
* Technical facts treated as proof of broader conclusions
* Correlation presented as causation
* Absence of evidence presented as evidence of absence
* One disputed specification treated as decisive
* Historical information presented as current
* Test configuration presented as operational configuration
* Prototype capability presented as production capability
Do not criticize an argument merely because it reaches a different conclusion.
Evaluate whether the conclusion is adequately supported by its stated evidence and criteria.
---
# 13. FACT-CHECK STATUS
Assign exactly ONE status to every claim.
### VERIFIED
Reliable evidence supports the claim.
### PARTIALLY VERIFIED
The general claim is supported, but one or more details are inaccurate, incomplete or overly broad.
### CONTRADICTED
Reliable evidence directly conflicts with the claim.
### UNVERIFIED
No sufficiently reliable public evidence was found.
### OUTDATED
The claim may have been historically correct but is no longer accurate for the relevant timeframe or configuration.
### CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
The claim may be correct under specific conditions, configuration, mission profile or definition, but is misleading when presented as universally true.
### VISUALLY PLAUSIBLE — NOT CONFIRMED
The footage suggests the claim, but reliable public evidence is insufficient to confirm it.
Do NOT label a claim FALSE merely because it cannot be verified.
---
# 14. CONCLUSION TYPE FOR CONTROVERSIAL CLAIMS
For the video's major conclusion, use exactly ONE:
### DIRECTLY SUPPORTED
The conclusion follows from well-established evidence and clearly defined criteria.
### PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
Important elements are supported, but the conclusion extends beyond what the evidence establishes.
### CONDITIONALLY VALID
The conclusion is valid only under a specific definition, configuration, timeframe or assumption.
### CONTESTED
Credible sources or technical interpretations materially disagree.
### UNSUPPORTED
The available evidence does not adequately support the conclusion.
### NOT DETERMINABLE FROM PUBLIC EVIDENCE
Available open-source information is insufficient to reach a reliable determination.
Do not treat disagreement between reasonable technical definitions as proof that one side is false.

# 19. OPERATIONAL VS THEORETICAL CAPABILITY
Explicitly distinguish between:
* Advertised capability
* Manufacturer-claimed capability
* Designed capability
* Tested capability
* Demonstrated capability
* Operational capability
* Combat-proven capability
Do not treat these as interchangeable.
For example:
A system being technically capable of performing an action does NOT automatically prove that it routinely performs that action operationally.

# 20. SOURCE QUALITY
For every important verification, assess source quality:
### HIGH
Primary, official, authoritative or directly documented evidence.
### MEDIUM
Reputable specialist source or independently corroborated secondary evidence.
### LOW
Weak, indirect, anonymous, poorly sourced or speculative evidence.
Source quality is NOT the same as evidence strength.
A high-quality source may still provide insufficient evidence for a specific claim.
---
# 21. EVIDENCE STRENGTH
For important claims, assign:
### VERY HIGH
Multiple authoritative or primary sources directly support the claim.
### HIGH
Strong authoritative or independently corroborated evidence exists.
### MODERATE
Credible evidence exists but important limitations remain.
### LOW
Evidence is indirect, incomplete or dependent on weaker sources.
### INSUFFICIENT
Available evidence does not permit a reliable determination.

# 27. FINAL ANALYTICAL RULE
Your task is NOT to prove the video wrong.
Your task is to establish the evidence.
Always ask:
**WHAT DOES THE VIDEO CLAIM?**
**WHAT DOES RELIABLE OPEN-SOURCE EVIDENCE SAY?**
**WHERE DO THEY AGREE?**
**WHERE DO THEY DISAGREE?**
**WHAT IS VARIANT / CONFIGURATION DEPENDENT?**
**WHAT IS TIME DEPENDENT?**
**WHAT IS DEFINITION DEPENDENT?**
**WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN?**
**DOES THE VIDEO'S CONCLUSION ACTUALLY FOLLOW FROM ITS EVIDENCE?**
Maintain a neutral, evidence-based approach.
Do not exaggerate errors.
Do not treat missing evidence as proof of falsehood.
Do not treat one weak source as definitive.
Do not treat multiple websites repeating the same source as independent confirmation.
Always distinguish:
**FACT**
**CLAIM**
**OBSERVATION**
**INFERENCE**
**INTERPRETATION**
**UNCERTAINTY**
The final report must allow the reader to independently determine exactly which parts of the video are:
* Technically supported
* Partially supported
* Contradicted
* Outdated
* Context-dependent
* Visually inconsistent
* Unverified
* Definition-dependent
* Logically unsupported
The final analysis must be evidence-led, technically precise, source-aware, variant-aware, configuration-aware and transparent about uncertainty.
